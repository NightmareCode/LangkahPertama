// Our Story

Named after the Sunda Shelf — the drowned continental shelf beneath our seas, one of the richest cradles of life on Earth.

Surin SuksuwanCo-founder · conservationist, 25+ years in biodiversity, formerly WWF-Malaysia
Dylan Jefri OngCo-founder · biodiversity consultant; met Surin on the Perlis State Park project, 2000

Two friends met in a state park in 2000. Each quietly dreamed of a bookshop full of nature books — and in 2022, with their own savings, they decided to build it together.

The doors opened on 24 June 2023, a bijou first-floor walk-up above the iconic On Line Pub. Inside: blond-pine shelves against gallery-white walls, rare Malaysiana in an armoire by the counter, a children's corner, and a café run by the family.

Day to day, the shop is kept by Jennifer Neoh Tan, while chef Sumalee Suksuwan fills it with the smell of hand-rolled curry puffs. More than a shop — it's a gathering place for conservationists, NGOs and the nature-curious.

// The Café · by Maleepap

Books pair well with curry puffs.

Chef Sumalee Suksuwan hand-rolls her famous karipap pusing in-house — reviewers call them "killer curry puffs."

Karipap Pusing · 01

The Original

Butter-crust spiral, chicken, potato & egg — the one that made the name.

Karipap Pusing · 02

Siamese Rendang

Slow, rich Siamese-style chicken rendang in a crisp spiral shell.

Karipap Pusing · 03

Sardine

The old-school classic, done properly.

Alongside: nasi lemak & bihun bungkus, Thai red curry, Vietnamese coffee, Thai milk tea, pandan tea — and old-school desserts like soya longan jelly and snow fungus soup. Come for a book, stay for lunch.

// More than a bookshop

Where nature people gather.

Sunda Talks

Weekend book launches, workshops and talks — from Mah Meri animal folklore to orchids, butterflies and Batu Arang history.

Nature Reads on BFM 89.9

The co-founders' recurring radio segment — 13 episodes and counting of books worth reading.

Conservation on the shelves

Art prints and mugs by Choo Beng Teong, turtle-motif batik supporting the Turtle Conservation Society — and out-of-print nature references rescued back into circulation.
